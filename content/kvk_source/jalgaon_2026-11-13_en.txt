Rabi sunflower Sowing Stage Sowing of the Rabi sunflower crop should be completed as early as possible.
                             Seed rate: 5 kg per hectare for dibbling (manual placement), or 8-10 kg per
                             hectare when using a seed drill. To prevent downy mildew and wilt diseases,
                             treat the seeds with 2-2.5 grams of Thiram or Captan per kilogram of seed.
                             Subsequently, apply 25 grams each of Azotobacter and Phosphate Solubilizing
                             Bacteria (PSB) or 10 ml of 'Phule Bio Mix' per kilogram of seed before
                             sowing. Sowing spacing : 45 cm × 30 cm for medium to deep soils; 60 cm ×
                             30 cm for heavy soils or when using hybrid varieties. Do not sow seeds deeper
                             than 5 cm. Fertilizer management : For rainfed (dryland) crops, apply 50 kg of
                             nitrogen, 25 kg of phosphorus, and 25 kg of potash per hectare. For irrigated
                             crops, apply 30 kg of nitrogen, 30 kg of phosphorus, and 30 kg of potash per
                             hectare at the time of sowing. The remaining 30 kg of nitrogen should be
                             applied one month after sowing (provided there is sufficient moisture in the
                             soil).
  Chickpea     Sowing Stage Sowing of dryland gram should be done when there is sufficient moisture in
                             the soil. Sustainable yields can be achieved by cultivating the 'Phule Vikram'
                             variety and using the sprinkler irrigation method. For irrigated gram, sowing
                             should be carried out as soon as possible. For sowing, select indigenous
                             chickpea (gram) varieties such as Vijay, Vishal, Digvijay, Phule Vikrant, and
                             Phule Vishwaraj, suitable for both rainfed and irrigated areas. Among Kabuli
                             chickpea varieties, choose from Virat, Krupa, PKV-2, PKV-4. For seed
                                treatment, use 6 grams of Trichoderma harzianum 1% WP per kilogram of
                                seeds. Subsequently, coat the seeds with 25 grams of Rhizobium culture and
                                PSB per kilogram or apply 10 ml of 'Phule Bio Mix' per kg of seeds. For the
                                integrated management of the pod borer, mix 200 grams of sorghum seeds
                                with the chickpea seeds before sowing. Sowing should be carried out with a
                                spacing of 30 cm x 10 cm. After sowing and before germination, while there is
                                still moisture in the soil, spray the herbicide pendimethalin 2.5 to 3 liters in
                                500 liters of water per hectare.

  Wheat      Sowing stage Sowing for irrigated crop should be carried out as soon as possible. Use
                          approximately 100-125 kg of seeds per hectare. Maintain a spacing of 20-22.5
                          cm between rows. Treat the seeds as follows: first, apply 3 grams of the
                          fungicide thiram per kilogram of seed; next, apply 17.5 ml of the insecticide

                          seeds.
Rabi Maize   Sowing stage Sowing should be carried out as soon as possible. Use 15-20 kg of seeds per
                          hectare. For medium and late-maturing varieties, maintain a spacing of 75 ×

                          25 grams of azotobacter per kilogram of seed or apply 10 ml of 'Phule Bio
                          Mix' per kg of seeds.

 Banana       Vegetative To control leaf spot (sigatoka/blight) disease in the plantation, farmers should
                 stage    prune and destroy the affected leaves. Subsequently, spray the crop with one
                          of the following combinations: a mixture of Metiram + Pyraclostrobin (3
                          grams per litre of water) plus 10 ml of mineral oil (Banole) per litre;
                          Azoxystrobin + Difenconazole (2 ml per litre of water); Tebuconazole +
                          Trifloxystrobin (0.5 ml per litre of water); or Tilt (Propiconazole) (0.5 ml per
                          litre of water) mixed with 10 ml of mineral oil per litre. In the fourth month
                          after planting, apply a foliar spray of micronutrients—EDTA-Zinc and EDTA-
                          Iron (50 grams of each dissolved in 10 litres of water). Provide irrigation to the
                          plantation based on availability and necessity, preferably using a drip
                          irrigation system. Schedule irrigation according to actual requirements by
                          using soil moisture sensors. It is best to irrigate the plantation during the night
                          or early morning hours. For mulching, place 30-micron thick, silver-coloured
                          polyethylene sheets in the open spaces between two rows of banana plants.
                          Alternatively, use organic mulch materials such as sugarcane trash, wheat
                          straw, or soybean residue between the rows. Apply recommended fertilizers
                          through the drip irrigation system, tailored to the specific growth stage of the
                          banana crop. Additionally, use an anti-transpirant by spraying Kaolin powder
                          (at a concentration of 80 grams per litre of water) at fifteen-day intervals.
                          Spray the plants with a solution of 180 mg of acetylsalicylic acid (dissolved in
                          ethanol) per 10 liters of water. For bunch management, remove the male bud
                          (banana flower) as soon as the bunch has fully emerged. Retain eight to nine
                          hands (tiers of fruit) on the bunch and remove the remaining lower hands.
                          Once the bunch has fully emerged—and again 15 days later—spray it with a
                          mixture of 50 grams of potassium dihydrogen phosphate and 100 grams of
                          urea dissolved in 10 liters of water. Alternatively, after removing the male
                          bud, spray the bunch with a solution of 200 grams of sulphate of potash in 10
                          liters of water; repeat this application 30 days later. Cover the bunch with
                          white, perforated (6% porosity) polyethylene bags. Install a fence around the
                          orchard. Depending on the condition of the tree, apply 250–500 grams of
                          neem cake per plant.

  Animal                     Fodder Management
Managemen                    1) If water availability is extremely limited, instead of cultivating fodder crops
      t                      over a large area, plan for fodder production in a smaller, restricted area under
                             protected irrigation to meet the needs of your livestock.
                             2) Consider fodder crops such as sorghum (jowar), pearl millet (bajra), maize,
                             berseem, or lucerne, taking into account available water, sowing time, soil
                             moisture, and local climatic conditions.
                             3) Anticipating potential fodder shortages, ensure proper planning for the
                             utilization of available green fodder, dry stalks (kadba), crop residues, and
                             other stored dry fodder.
                             4) Focus on the conservation of available fodder, the cultivation of drought-
                             tolerant (low-water-consuming) fodder crops, the production of silage, and
                             enhancing the nutritional value of the fodder.
                             5) Instead of providing all available green fodder to the livestock at once,
                             distribute it in a planned and regulated manner.
                             6) Utilize the residues obtained from crops like soybean, sorghum, pearl
                             millet, maize, and groundnut as fodder.
                             7) Properly chop crop residues and store them in a safe, secure location to help
                             mitigate the severity of any future fodder shortages.
